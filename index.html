01 — ABOUT

A bench built on patience

I trained at the British Horological Institute in Upton Hall before spending eight years maintaining turret clocks for parish churches across the Pennines — the kind of work where a fault ignored becomes a fault the whole village hears. In 2015 I opened my own bench above a mill in Hebden Bridge, and I've been there since, splitting my week between restoration commissions and a small run of skeleton clocks built under my own name.

Every movement that comes through the door gets stripped to its plates, cleaned by hand, and rebuilt with parts made to match — not replace — what came before. I keep the tools my instructors used, and a few that belonged to theirs.

A clock doesn't need to be modern to be right. It needs to be true.

02 — THE CRAFT

Broken into its parts, like everything else here

COMPONENT 01

Restoration

Full movement strip-down, cleaning, bushing and regulation for longcase, bracket, mantel and carriage clocks. Cases are treated with equal care — veneer repair, French polishing, and brass re-lacquering where the original finish allows.

COMPONENT 02

Custom Movements

Bespoke skeleton and carriage clocks built to commission. Hand-cut wheels, hand-engraved plates, and a visible escapement if you want the mechanism to be part of the design.

COMPONENT 03

Consultation

Condition reports, valuations and buying advice for auction-bound, inherited or uncertain clocks — an hour on the bench before you commit to a purchase or a repair.

COMPONENT 04

Maintenance

Annual servicing plans that keep a working clock keeping true time: oiling, depthing checks, and a regulation visit timed to the clock, not the calendar.

04 — PROCESS

How a clock leaves this bench

  1. 1

    Intake & Assessment

    A condition report, photographs, and a conversation about scope and budget before anything is opened.

  2. 2

    Strip & Clean

    Full disassembly and hand cleaning of every component — no ultrasonic shortcuts on movements this old.

  3. 3

    Repair & Replace

    Bushing worn pivots, re-cutting damaged teeth, and replacing only what genuinely can't be saved.

  4. 4

    Reassemble & Oil

    Correct period oils, precise depthing, and careful timing of the whole train before it's closed up.

  5. 5

    Regulate & Test

    A two-week bench test, adjusted until it holds ±2 seconds a day before it goes back to you.
